WAI · TIKANGA · FRESHWATER GOVERNANCE

Understand the context
of your wai.

There is no single universal set of “Māori water rules”. Tikanga and the relationships of mana whenua to a water body are local. National freshwater planning direction and drinking-water supplier duties have different legal roles.

For a supplier or project

Identify the water body and relevant mana whenua; check the current regional policy statement, freshwater plan, consent and any settlement arrangements with the council; engage appropriately about local tikanga and culturally sensitive information. Confirm drinking-water requirements separately with Taumata Arowai when unclear.

Source roleWhat it saysRead the source
Current national directionThe NPS-FM 2020, consolidated with December 2025 amendments, is in force. It directs councils under the Resource Management Act; clause 1.3 sets the Te Mana o te Wai framework and hierarchy.MfE · NPS-FM current instrument
How it works locallyThe Ministry's clause 1.3 guidance explains six principles, the health of wai, drinking water in the hierarchy, and regional council engagement with tangata whenua. It is explanatory guidance; read the instrument and regional plan for the applicable provisions.MfE · NOF clause 1.3 guidance
Tikanga in water safetyWater Safety NZ's Wai Puna account connects tikanga, whakapapa and mātauranga to whānau safety around wai. It is a water safety framework, not a drinking-water compliance instrument.Water Safety NZ · Wai Puna
Hapū and iwi interestsTe Wai Māori presents a perspective on mana, custom and freshwater rights. Its FAQ does not decide legal rights in a particular catchment.Te Wai Māori · rights FAQ (2019)
Historical discussionPita Sharples' 2009 forum speech discusses Māori relationships with wai and a stronger governance role. Its historical proposals and descriptions are not the current legal test.Beehive · 2009 speech
Separate supplier legislationTaumata Arowai explains that the direct Water Services Act duty for suppliers to give effect to Te Mana o te Wai was removed in 2025. Drinking-water safety duties still apply.Taumata Arowai · legislation update

The RMA also addresses the relationship of Māori with ancestral water and kaitiakitanga in sections 6–8; check the current Act. Treat data about wai with attention to authority, provenance, sensitivity and kaitiaki interests, guided by government Māori data and AI guidance.
