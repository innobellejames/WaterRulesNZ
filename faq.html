FREQUENT QUESTIONS

Start with a
practical question.

Open an answer for its source, or use it to ask a more specific question about your supply.

Which instrument tells me what applies?

Start with the Water Services Act and Drinking Water Standards Regulations; identify your supply and compliance pathway in the current Quality Assurance Rules or an applicable Acceptable Solution. Record how your specific source, treatment and network affect the answer.

Act and Rules
When do the 2026 Quality Assurance Rules start?

The regulator states that the 2022 Rules (Revised 2024) remain current until 1 July 2027; check its transition page before planning changes.

Taumata Arowai transition update
How do I prepare a drinking water safety plan?

Describe the supply from catchment to consumer, identify hazards and controls, plan monitoring, corrective action and review. Use the regulator's supply-size guidance and templates.

Safety planning guidance
Can any laboratory test my compliance samples?

Use a laboratory accredited for the relevant drinking water tests and methods. Check the laboratory's current scope in the IANZ directory, alongside Taumata Arowai's instructions.

Accredited lab guidance
Where can I find an accredited laboratory?

Search the IANZ accredited organisations directory and confirm the analyte and method are in the scope; accreditation of a lab alone is not a blanket approval for every test.

IANZ directory
What is HineKōrako?

It is the regulator's supplier portal for registering and managing supplies and providing information; its public registers can be browsed separately.

HineKōrako
What if I operate a bore?

Use regional bore records to locate and understand the source, inspect and protect the borehead, and assess hazards and test results. A mapped well is not proof of potability.

Safety planning guidance
Is the fluoride MAV a fluoridation target?

No. The 1.5 mg/L MAV is a health-based upper limit; decisions to fluoridate named supplies are made under separate public health powers. The Ministry's evidence review and the NTP systematic review address benefits and different exposure levels.

Drinking Water Standards
What do nitrate results mean for a bore supply?

Check whether the report uses nitrate (NO₃) or nitrate-nitrogen (NO₃-N). The short-term MAV is 50 mg/L as NO₃ or 11.3 mg/L as NO₃-N. Test and assess your source; boiling does not remove nitrate.

Taumata Arowai · nitrate facts
What if E. coli is detected?

Act promptly to protect consumers, issue the appropriate notice, investigate and correct contamination, and follow the duties applicable to your registered supply. A repeat test alone does not make water safe.

Taumata Arowai · response guidance
How do Boil Water, Do Not Drink and Do Not Use notices differ?

Boil Water addresses a possible microbial risk. Do Not Drink may involve chemicals or toxins, so boiling will not make it safe to consume. Do Not Use means avoid all affected tap-water use, including bathing. Follow the issuing supplier's notice.

Taumata Arowai · notice types
What should I do with a flooded bore or rainwater tank?

Do not use water contaminated by floodwater, silt, fuel or chemicals. Use safe alternative water, follow the bore or rainwater factsheet, and arrange accredited testing. Registered suppliers must notify the regulator when water is or may be unsafe.

Taumata Arowai · flood guidance
How often do suppliers report monitoring results?

The current reporting guidance lists Level 1 annual reporting, Level 2 quarterly monitoring plus annual assurance, and Level 3 monthly monitoring plus annual assurance. Very small community rules have no routine report in that table. Identify your actual modules and pathway before applying a period.

DWQAR reporting guidance · PDF
Can I submit all test results through the HineKōrako webform?

No. The current webform guide says it covers Level 1 and 2 Rules reporting but does not receive sample or monitoring results. Excel upload or a supported API can be used for those results; Level 3 uses Excel or API.

HineKōrako webform guide · PDF
Does chlorine taste mean the water is unsafe?

Taste alone cannot establish safety. Chlorine provides disinfection and a network residual; the health MAV, operating residual and aesthetic value are different measures. Investigate sudden changes and do not compromise disinfection to remove taste.

Taumata Arowai · chlorine
Where are my council's water notices?

Search your council in our water page finder, then check that council or its named water organisation for current notices and outages. If you do not know the supplier, use the public drinking water supply register first. Notices can be local and time-sensitive; verify their status with the issuing supplier.

What did the 2025 water services legislation add?

The Local Government (Water Services) Act 2025 is part of the current framework for local water service delivery and economic arrangements. Read the latest consolidated Act alongside the 2021 drinking water duties; these are different statutes.

2025 Act
Who can clarify an unclear national requirement?

Ask Taumata Arowai about its drinking water guidance. For a local project, asset or service notice, ask the relevant council or water organisation. For a possible unsafe supply, use the regulator's notification guidance promptly.

Taumata Arowai contact
Does Te Mana o te Wai still apply to freshwater management?

Yes. Clause 1.3 of the current NPS-FM includes its principles and hierarchy, directing regional council freshwater planning and engagement with tangata whenua. The separate Water Services Act duty for drinking-water suppliers to give effect to Te Mana o te Wai was removed in 2025. Check local plans and consents for a particular source or project.

MfE · current NPS-FM
Are tikanga or hapū and iwi rights the same everywhere?

No. Local relationships and tikanga need to be understood with the relevant mana whenua. Water Safety NZ explains tikanga in a whānau water safety framework; Te Wai Māori sets out a perspective on customary interests. Neither page decides a supplier's legal duties or title to a particular water body.

Te Wai Māori · rights perspective
What do the latest EHiNZ drinking-water indicators measure?

Its February 2026 update reports 2024 population estimates for registered supply access and three core safety barriers, plus September 2025 recorded and adjusted fluoridation access. These are surveillance estimates, not a live finding about a particular tap or today's fluoridation status.

EHiNZ · February 2026 update
How can I check whether my town is fluoridated?

Identify the actual supply and distribution zone, check the Ministry of Health's named-supply direction and progress page, and ask the supplier or council for current operation and measured results. Fluoride Free NZ's town and council lists are advocacy-maintained leads, not official confirmation; their council list can conflict with the Ministry's current page.

Ministry of Health · supply directions

If these sources do not resolve your situation, contact Taumata Arowai. For council-specific gaps, open that council's water page in the directory.
